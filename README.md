## What Is Considered a Dental Emergency?

Not every toothache requires emergency treatment, but certain symptoms should not be ignored. Severe or persistent tooth pain, significant facial or gum swelling, uncontrolled oral bleeding, a knocked-out permanent tooth, serious dental trauma, or a tooth that has fractured close to the gumline may require urgent evaluation.

An infection is another concern. A dental abscess can cause pain, swelling, tenderness, and sometimes fever or a general feeling of illness. When swelling spreads toward the face, jaw, or neck, the situation may become more serious and should receive prompt medical attention.

The key question is not simply how painful the problem feels. It is whether delaying treatment could lead to additional damage, infection, or complications.

## When Should You Seek Immediate Dental Care?

A practical rule is to seek professional advice when dental pain is severe, continues despite basic measures, or is accompanied by swelling, fever, bleeding, or trauma.

A knocked-out permanent tooth is particularly time-sensitive. If this happens, handle the tooth carefully by the crown rather than the root, avoid scrubbing it, and seek dental care as quickly as possible. For facial injuries involving difficulty breathing, swallowing, or significant bleeding, an emergency department may be appropriate.

For less severe problems, calling a dental office can help determine how quickly you need to be seen. A dental professional can assess your symptoms and recommend the appropriate next step rather than leaving you to decide based only on online information.

## Why Treating the Cause Matters

Pain medication may temporarily reduce discomfort, but it does not necessarily resolve the dental condition causing the pain. Depending on the diagnosis, treatment may involve repairing a damaged tooth, treating an infection, performing a root canal, draining an abscess, or removing a tooth that cannot be restored.

The ADA notes that antibiotics are not appropriate for most dental pain and certain localized dental conditions without systemic involvement. Dental treatment that addresses the source of the problem is often the more appropriate approach. Antibiotics may be considered when symptoms indicate systemic involvement, such as fever or malaise.

This is one reason an urgent dental evaluation can be more useful than simply trying to control symptoms at home.

These figures highlight the importance of having a local dental provider you can contact when an unexpected problem occurs.

## What Should You Do Before Your Appointment?

While waiting for professional care, avoid placing aspirin directly against the gums or attempting to repair a broken tooth yourself. If a tooth has been knocked out, keep it moist and seek immediate professional guidance. For swelling, a cold compress applied externally may help with discomfort.

If there is severe bleeding, significant facial trauma, trouble breathing or swallowing, or rapidly increasing swelling, do not delay emergency medical evaluation.

It is also helpful to provide the dental office with clear information about when the problem began, what caused it, whether swelling or fever is present, and any medications you are currently taking. This allows the dental team to better understand the situation before examination.
